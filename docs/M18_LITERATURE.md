# Milestone 18: related work and novelty (phase 1b)

Compiled on 2026-11-25 from opened primary pages, about 50 papers. A research sub-agent did the search and reading. Each row is labelled FT (full text read), AO (abstract only) or MD (metadata only).

The entries that decide the novelty verdicts were re-checked by hand on 2026-11-25:

* **RoadWeaver** (arXiv 2608.11580, 12 Aug 2026), from its arXiv HTML method section:
  * a learned skeleton (VQ-VAE + conditional masked transformer);
  * procedural local-road growth guided by a structure tensor field;
  * dangling endpoints reconnected by A\* over a cost map, then largest-component filtering;
  * no terrain or elevation.
* **Fang et al. 2022** (IJGIS 36(10):2035–2059), from the author PDF:
  * GAN inpainting of missing street segments within a region;
  * conditioned on the surrounding street network and 7 topographic channels (elevation, slope, aspect, 4 hillshades);
  * about 56,000 raster samples from Florence, Perugia, Rome and Siena.
* **CityStack and RoBus.** The baselines search (`docs/M18_BASELINES.md`) reached the same facts independently: CityStack does not implement terrain or water, and RoBus has no DEM.

**Legend for the last column.** FT = full text opened and the relevant sections read (method, data, evaluation). AO = ABSTRACT ONLY (abstract or landing page; the full text was not read). MD = metadata only (title, authors and venue verified, no abstract available). "n.v." = not verified.

**Three kinds of work** (tagged in the "Kind" column of the matrix):
- **GEN**: terrain-conditioned (or terrain-aware) *network* generation. A network grows over an area. This is UrbanMorph's category.
- **ROUTE**: routing a road between *supplied* points or settlements over terrain (least-cost or anisotropic shortest path).
- **EMP**: empirical analysis of real networks.
- Other tags: **GEN-noT** = network generation without terrain; **CITY** = city/layout generators; **ML** = general inference-time control methods.

## 2. Candidate contributions: novelty verdicts

**C1. Network-aware inference (closed-dead-end and closed-island potentials that telescope exactly, moment-matched λ, look-ahead/SMC) on a learned terrain-conditioned vector road generator. Verdict: narrowly novel.**
The machinery is not new. Exponentially tilting a frozen generator with moment-matched λ is GDC (Khalifa et al. 2021). SMC or twisted SMC over frozen autoregressive or diffusion models is Lew et al. 2023, Zhao et al. 2024 and Wu et al. 2023. Writing a global potential as a sum of per-step increments is potential-based shaping (Ng et al. 1999). Hard-constraint projection during graph generation is PRODIGY and ConStruct. What I found no precedent for is applying this to road-graph growth with *closed* (irreversible) dead-end and island counts. Because closed features cannot reopen, the increments are final and no twist has to be learned. The empirical result (about 70% less same-land OD unreachability on unseen cities at matched realism) is also unprecedented as far as I found.
- **Strongest counterexample: RoadWeaver (arXiv 2608.11580, Aug 2026).** It is a learned road-network generator that explicitly targets and reports dead-end ratio, reachability and LCC. It fixes connectivity by post-hoc A\* reconnection over the learned road-field cost plus LCC filtering, not by inference-time potentials, and it uses no terrain.
- **Second counterexample:** DRoLaS's "connectivity refinement strategy" (details n.v.).
- **Positioning:** present the telescoping as an instance of Ng et al. 1999 and the λ fit as GDC-style moment matching, and claim the closure property plus the application and the results. The negative result on adaptive look-ahead is worth keeping.

**C2. DEM-conditioned learned vector road growth with leakage controls. Verdict: narrowly novel.**
Learned DEM-conditioned *urban* street generation exists: Fang et al. 2022 (7 DEM-derived channels, 512 m tiles, Italian hill towns; Fang et al. 2020 is a precursor). Learned DEM-to-*vector road graph* exists for non-urban roads: Zhou et al. 2024 (elevation, water and vegetation maps → density map → transformer). The remaining novelty has three parts:
- autoregressive NTG-style vector growth conditioned on terrain only (no surrounding streets, no junction hints, no city label);
- class and structure (ground/bridge/tunnel) heads;
- **explicit leakage controls for street imprints in high-resolution LiDAR DEMs.** None of the papers I opened addresses this. A keyword search of both Fang papers found no "leak", "imprint" or "LiDAR"; Fang uses a 30 m DEM, where imprints are less of a concern.
- **Strongest counterexample: Fang et al. 2022.** It is learned, urban, DEM-conditioned and at the same tile scale. It differs in being raster inpainting that receives context streets and junction heat maps, and it is evaluated only by pixel MAE/MSE.

**C3. Evaluation of the realism vs terrain-adaptation trade-off (TAS, named steep sites). Verdict: narrowly novel (probably novel as a protocol).**
I found no paper that scores generated street networks jointly on network realism and terrain adaptation (grade, contour following, siting) and reports a trade-off on named steep sites. Fang 2022 compares hilly and flat areas only by pixel error. Each ingredient exists separately:
- empirical street grades for 8,914 urban areas (Boeing 2021);
- relief-circuity correlation (Yang et al. 2021);
- contour-following as a design rule (Chen et al. 2008; Nishida et al.; Parish & Müller's "San Francisco rule");
- metric-dependent trade-offs between morphologies (Dobbie & Johansson 2026 / CitySprout; RoBus 2026 also reports a fidelity vs connectivity trade-off).

**Strongest counterexample: Dobbie & Johansson 2026**, which makes the "no single optimum, trade-off depends on the metric" argument for generated networks, but without terrain. To strengthen C3, TAS should be computed on the real hilly networks (with the same DEM) to show where real cities sit on the trade-off.

**C4. Diagnosis of excess close junctions and online topology-aware junction reuse. Verdict: not novel as a mechanism; narrowly novel at most as a diagnosis.**
Snapping new nodes to existing nodes within a radius is standard:
- Parish & Müller 2001 (snap to crossings within a radius);
- **NTG itself (5 m merge)**;
- Nishida et al. (vertex merge);
- CitySprout (`intersectRadius`);
- RoadWeaver (snapping radius);
- Galin 2011 (path merging into junctions).

Making the merge *topology-aware* mirrors Boeing 2025's topological consolidation (merge only nodes connected along the network within a tolerance), there used for measurement. **Strongest counterexample: NTG's 5 m merge rule**, in the very model UrbanMorph extends.

**Caveat for the diagnosis:** real OSM networks themselves overcount intersections (median +14% without consolidation, Boeing 2025; divided roads and roundabouts create clusters of close nodes). The "excess" is only meaningful if real and generated graphs are consolidated identically (same tolerance and method) before comparison.

**C5. Hybrid: learned arterial backbone + procedural local infill on terrain. Verdict: not novel in concept.**
- **Strongest counterexample: RoadWeaver (Aug 2026)**: a learned global skeleton, then procedural tensor-field growth of local roads, then reconnection. No terrain.
- Nishida et al. 2016: arterials first, local streets seeded along arterials, with terrain-slope rules; example-based rather than learned.
- Birsak et al. 2022: P1 roads condition P2.
- Galin 2011 and J. Beneš 2014: procedural major-then-minor over terrain.
- Chen 2008: major and minor tensor fields with a height map.
- Sun et al. 2026: road skeleton first.

Only the specific pairing of a *terrain-conditioned learned* backbone with *terrain-aware* procedural infill is unoccupied, and RoadWeaver plus Nishida together nearly cover it. Present it as engineering, not as a contribution.

## 3. Urban-science measurement notes

- **Block area/shape.** Use Louf & Barthelemy's block area A and Φ = A/A_circumscribed-circle, and compare the conditional P(Φ | A) rather than the marginals (Louf & Barthelemy 2014). Blocks ignore dead ends, so pair block statistics with a dead-end metric. On real OSM data, filter face artifacts first: dual carriageways give large elongated faces, and roundabouts and turning lanes give small compact faces (Fleischmann & Vybornova 2024). Otherwise real block-area distributions carry spurious mass that single-centreline generated graphs never produce.
- **Orientation.** Follow Boeing 2019: bidirectional bearings, 36 bins of 10°, entropy H in nats (maximum 3.584, ideal 4-way grid 1.386), orientation order φ normalised between those two values and squared. Report unsimplified length-weighted and simplified unweighted variants; the weighted one is biased by city shape. On steep sites, orientation tends to follow contours (Chen 2008 and Nishida 2016 use this as a generation rule). **I found no empirical paper that directly measures street-contour alignment** in real cities, so TAS's contour term has no published empirical baseline.
- **Intersection consolidation.** Boeing 2025: overcounting comes from nonplanarity, intersection complexity (a divided-road crossing gives 4 nodes; a roundabout gives 4 three-way nodes) and curve digitisation. Topological consolidation (merge only network-connected nodes within a tolerance) is preferred; the median urban-area overcount without it is 14%. Boeing 2021 uses 10 m buffers; Barrington-Leigh & Millard-Ball 2019 merge intersections within 20 m and anneal degree-2 nodes.
  - **Implication for UrbanMorph:** consolidate real and generated graphs identically before computing junction spacing, degree or S10. Report sensitivity at 10/15/20 m, since the online reuse threshold is 15 m.
  - **Offset (staggered) junctions:** I found no paper that quantifies them. That consolidation merges offsets shorter than the tolerance into one node follows from the algorithm description, not from a reported measurement.
- **Disconnectedness.** SNDi is the first principal component of nodal degree, dead-end share, 4+-way share, dendricity (bridges, dead ends, self-loops, cycle-basis share), circuity and sinuosity, on a roughly 1 km grid over urban cells only (Barrington-Leigh & Millard-Ball 2019). It includes footpaths and service roads (2020). Two consequences:
  - D and I counts plus OD unreachability are narrower than SNDi. Adding bridge share (dendricity) and circuity would make the results comparable to it.
  - If UrbanMorph drops paths and service roads, absolute dead-end rates are not comparable with SNDi numbers.
  - "Transport convenience" (NTG; RoBus uses the same Euclid/Dijkstra ratio as "traffic convenience") is undefined for unreachable pairs. State how unreachable OD pairs are scored.
- **Slope effects.**
  - Hilly cities have the most circuitous networks: Caracas, Hong Kong and Sarajevo are 13.3–14.8% more circuitous than straight-line distance, "due largely to topography" (Boeing 2019).
  - Relief correlates most strongly with circuity among complexity metrics in Chinese cities (Yang et al. 2021).
  - Terrain variation and altitude are principal components of global street form (Zhou, Bao & Liu 2022).
  - Boeing 2021 publishes per-edge grades from about 30 m ASTER/SRTM DEMs, the natural empirical reference for TAS's gentle-grade term. Grades from 30 m DEMs are smoothed relative to UrbanMorph's LiDAR (4 m) grades, so do not compare absolute values without resampling to a common DEM.
- **Hierarchy.** Marshall 2005 is the standard vocabulary (street type and hierarchy, route structure). OSM class labels are noisy, and errors appear as gaps and disconnected parts within a hierarchy level (Guth et al. 2021). Class-conditioned realism statistics therefore inherit label noise. Louf, Jensen & Barthelemy 2013 show that hierarchy can emerge from cost-benefit growth, a possible baseline for the arterial backbone.
