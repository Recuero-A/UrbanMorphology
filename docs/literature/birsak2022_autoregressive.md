## 3. What it does

A learned generator produces city-scale street networks of about 20 km × 20 km and up. It works in three stages:

1. A VQ-VAE compresses distance-field images of street graphs into small index fields.
2. A conditional transformer generates these indices autoregressively in a sliding window. The conditions are a street-density map, the high-priority (P1) road network and a land/water map.
3. The VQ-VAE decoder and thinning plus road tracing turn the result back into a vector street graph.

The paper generates the low-priority streets (P2) given the P1 roads. Terrain is explicitly not used.

## 4. How it generates

- **Learned.**
  - Trained on OpenStreetMap from the 50 largest US cities.
  - 1–4 A100 GPUs: about 24 h for the VQ-VAE and 48 h for the transformer ("IndexModel").
- **Representation: raster internally, vector output.**
  - Each 1223 m crop (OSM zoom 15) is rendered as a 256×256 distance field.
  - It is encoded into 16×16 indices (cells of 76.44 m).
  - It is decoded back to a distance field, then thresholded, thinned, road-traced and filtered of small disconnected parts. The output is a vector graph.
- **Algorithm (§3):**
  1. **Train the CompressionModel.** A VQGAN without the discriminator, using reconstruction, perceptual and dictionary losses, on distance fields. Distance fields worked better than one-pixel line drawings, which fragmented.
  2. **Train the IndexModel.** An encoder–decoder transformer with a 16×16-cell window (sequence 256), next-token prediction p(sᵢ | s<ᵢ, c). The context c combines:
     - a cell context: quantised density through a linear layer;
     - a pixel context: patches of the P1 distance field and the land/water map, flattened through a linear layer.
  3. **Inference.**
     - Slide the window one cell at a time over a 256×256-cell index field (about 19.5 km × 19.5 km in Mercator space).
     - Decode, threshold, thin, trace (RoadTracer-style), and filter small components.
  4. **Priority classes (App. B).**
     - P1: motorway, trunk, primary, secondary and their links.
     - P2: tertiary(+link), residential, living_street, road, unclassified.
     - Service roads and pedestrian ways are excluded.

## 5. Inputs, outputs and how terrain enters

- **Inputs (conditions):**
  - street-density map (street length per area, from a global map at 19.11 m resolution);
  - P1 street distance field;
  - land/water map.
- **Outputs:** P2 street graph (vector).
- **Terrain: none.** The authors state they do not condition on terrain, because training data were hard to obtain.
- **Water:** enters only through the land/water condition map.
- **No grade limits.**

## 6. Evaluation

- **Diversity (Fig. 6).** Two samples per condition set for three US cities, 9.8 km crops, with insets against ground truth.
- **Large scale.** About 20 km generations (New York, Fig. 1), using the **ground-truth density map** "to allow a direct comparison with existing cities".
- **Style transfer.** Istanbul regenerated by the US-trained model (Fig. 7).
- **Statistics vs CityEngine (Fig. 8 and Fig. 9, App. C–D).** Histograms for Chicago, San Francisco, Washington DC and Los Angeles of:
  - fraction of street length by angle;
  - node degree;
  - segment circuity;
  - transport ratio.
  Ground truth, the model and CityEngine are overlaid. No scalar distances or tests are reported in the PDF; more is said to be in the supplementary material, which I did not find.
- **CityEngine baseline setup (App. C).** Block width and length sampled from the ground-truth city, an obstacle map for water, 50,000 streets, cropped.
- **GANs.** StyleGAN2 outputs are shown as unusable after thinning (Fig. 5).
- **Not reported:** a user study, or generation runtime per km².
- **Metric definitions (App. D):**
  - a *segment* is a chain of edges through degree-2 vertices;
  - *circuity* = segment length / straight-line distance between the segment ends;
  - *transport ratio* = graph distance / Euclidean distance, sampled over n = 500 random vertex pairs;
  - *PageRank* with k = 0.85, plus "Pagerank-by-edge" on the edge graph with k = 0.95;
  - densities are normalised by land area.
  - **Caveat:** the streetz README defines segment circuity the other way up (Euclidean / length). Pin the definition before reusing the code.

## 8. Comparability with UrbanMorph

- **Not runnable.** No generator code or weights were found. Only the evaluation code (streetz, Apache-2.0) is public.
- **Matched inputs (if reimplemented).**
  - *Water mask:* is the land/water map.
  - *Arterials:* our supplied major roads are P1.
  - *Density map:* must be supplied identically to all methods. Ground-truth density would leak.
  - *Missing:* there is no DEM channel. Adding one would make it our model, not theirs.
  - *Scale:* our 512 m tile is smaller than their 1223 m crop.
- **Effort (my estimate, labelled).** 80–150 h plus training: VQ-VAE, transformer, sliding window, thinning and tracing, OSM pipeline. M18_BASELINES gives a similar figure for comparable learned raster pipelines (CaRoLS: 80–150 h). Not recommended as a baseline.
- **Fair comparison (in principle):** **completion given arterials** (P2 given P1). This matches our class-conditioned growth.

## 9. Ideas we could adopt for evaluation

- **Reuse the streetz metrics** (Apache-2.0): circuity, transport ratio, node degree, length-weighted orientation, block statistics, PageRank and betweenness. Fix the circuity direction explicitly, given the paper–README mismatch.
- **Leakage-aware conditioning.** Their large-scale results use the ground-truth density map. In our protocol, state for every method which ground-truth-derived inputs it receives (arterials, density, entry points), and give them identically. This is our "matched inputs" rule.
- **Numbers, not only histograms.** They compare to CityEngine by overlaid histograms. We should report a distance per statistic (e.g. Wasserstein-1 or KS) to the real distribution on the same tile, with seed variance.
- **Two-class evaluation.** P1/P2 splitting is a clean precedent for evaluating local streets given real arterials separately from the full growth.
- **Small-component filtering is a post-hoc fix.** Like RoadWeaver's repair, it hides disconnection. Report connectivity metrics **before** any filtering for all methods.
