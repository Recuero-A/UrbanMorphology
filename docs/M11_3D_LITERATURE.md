# Milestone 11 — Literature: procedural 3D city and building generation

Written 2026-11-01. Bibliographic details were checked against publisher, Crossref, CVF or author pages,
listed per entry. **Not a systematic review.** Papers whose details could not be verified are marked as such.

## 1. Core procedural-modeling papers

### 1.1 Parish & Müller (2001), *Procedural Modeling of Cities*

* **Citation.** Y. I. H. Parish, P. Müller. SIGGRAPH 2001, pp. 301–308. doi:10.1145/383259.383292.
  Checked against the ACM SIGGRAPH History Archives and the DOI record.
* **Method.**
  * Extended L-systems grow a road network, steered by global goals (population density, road patterns) and
    local constraints (water, elevation).
  * Blocks are split into lots, and each lot gets a building generated by a second L-system.
  * Façades come from procedural textures, not geometry.
* **Relevance to M11.**
  * Our streets and lots come from M10 (GraphTurtle + BF), so only the **lot → building** stage is relevant.
  * This paper establishes the "2D layout first, then 3D buildings per lot" split that M11 also uses.

### 1.2 Wonka, Wimmer, Sillion & Ribarsky (2003), *Instant Architecture*

* **Citation.** P. Wonka, M. Wimmer, F. Sillion, W. Ribarsky. *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 22(3), pp. 669–677
  (SIGGRAPH 2003). doi:10.1145/882262.882324. Checked on Crossref.
* **Method.**
  * **Split grammars:** a façade is recursively split into floors, then tiles, then windows and doors, along
    the façade's own axes.
  * A **control grammar** distributes attributes such as style and material consistently over the building.
* **Relevance.**
  * The direct model for our façade system, which uses hierarchical splits instead of random window placement.
  * The control grammar shows how to get controlled variation: one style per building or block, varied within
    limits.

### 1.3 Müller, Wonka, Haegler, Ulmer & Van Gool (2006), *Procedural Modeling of Buildings*

* **Citation.** P. Müller, P. Wonka, S. Haegler, A. Ulmer, L. Van Gool. *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 25(3),
  pp. 614–623. doi:10.1145/1141911.1141931. Checked on a library catalogue record and researchr.
* **Method: CGA shape.**
  * Mass modelling from simple volumes.
  * A **component split** turns a volume into its faces: front, sides, roof.
  * **Split and repeat** rules lay out the façade.
  * **Occlusion queries** stop windows being placed where another volume or a neighbouring building touches
    the wall.
* **Relevance.**
  * The operations we need are a small subset: `comp(faces)`, `split(y){floors}`, `repeat(x){tile}`, and
    an occlusion test.
  * The occlusion test matters for us: European blocks have **attached buildings with shared party walls**,
    where windows must not appear.

## 2. Roofs and footprint geometry

### 2.1 Aichholzer, Aurenhammer, Alberts & Gärtner (1995), *A Novel Type of Skeleton for Polygons*

* **Citation.** *Journal of Universal Computer Science* 1(12), pp. 752–761. doi:10.3217/jucs-001-12-0752.
  Checked on the J.UCS and TU Graz pages.
* **The idea.** The **straight skeleton** is the canonical way to build a hip roof over any simple polygon:
  every wall gets one roof plane of equal slope.
* **What we can use.**
  * No straight-skeleton implementation is available in our environment: Shapely 2.2 has none, and there is
    no CGAL.
  * The first prototype builds hip and gable roofs analytically for (near-)rectangular footprints only, and
    falls back to flat roofs elsewhere (`M11_PROCEDURAL_BUILDINGS.md`).

### 2.2 Kelly & Wonka (2011), *Interactive Architectural Modeling with Procedural Extrusions*

* **Citation.** T. Kelly, P. Wonka. *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 30(2), article 14, pp. 1–15.
  doi:10.1145/1944846.1944854. Checked on Crossref.
* **Method.** A *weighted* straight skeleton with per-edge profiles produces roofs, mansards and setbacks over
  arbitrary footprints.
* **Relevance.** This is the general solution for complex roofs. It is deferred until simple roofs on simple
  footprints are validated.

## 3. Levels of detail and heights from 2D data

### 3.1 Biljecki, Ledoux & Stoter (2016), *An Improved LOD Specification for 3D Building Models*

* **Citation.** *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* 59, pp. 25–37.
  doi:10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2016.04.005. Checked on TU Delft pages.
* **Content.** 16 refined LODs, from LOD0 (footprint) through LOD1.x (block models) and LOD2.x (roof shapes)
  to LOD3.x (openings in the façade).
* **Relevance.** Gives a vocabulary for what we generate and evaluate:
  * prototype 1 is **LOD1.2 / LOD2.0** (extruded footprints with simple roofs);
  * prototype 2 adds LOD3-like façade openings, but these are *procedural*, not observed.
  * Renders must be labelled with the LOD actually produced.

### 3.2 Biljecki, Ledoux & Stoter (2017), *Generating 3D City Models without Elevation Data*

* **Citation.** *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* 64, pp. 1–18.
  doi:10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2017.01.001. Checked on the TU Delft repository.
* **Method.** Predicts building heights from 2D footprint attributes with random-forest regression, then
  extrudes the footprints.
* **Relevance.** Precedent for a learned height model later. M11 starts with a transparent procedural baseline,
  as the specification asks.

### 3.3 Milojevic-Dupont et al. (2020), *Learning from Urban Form to Predict Building Heights*

* **Citation.** *PLOS ONE* 15(12), e0242010. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0242010. Checked on DOAJ and the TU Berlin
  repository.
* **Finding.** The shape of the surrounding urban fabric is a strong predictor of a building's height, and the
  error transferred to an unseen region falls below typical floor height.
* **Relevance.** Supports using **neighbourhood context** (block, density, neighbours' heights) rather than
  independent per-building sampling. This is the specification's spatial-correlation requirement.

## 4. Recent 3D city and world generation

| Work | Verified details | Representation | Relevance to us |
|---|---|---|---|
| CityDreamer (Xie, Chen, Hong, Liu) | CVPR 2024; arXiv:2309.00610 (CVF poster page) | Separate generators for building instances and background (roads, green), on top of a generated layout; neural rendering | Supports splitting layout from building generation. Its outputs are neural fields, not editable meshes, so it cannot be explored with collision in Godot. |
| InfiniCity | ICCV 2023; arXiv:2301.09637 (author list **not verified**) | Generates a 2D map, then lifts it to 3D with a completion module | Same "2D layout → 3D lifting" order as our pipeline. |
| Infinigen (Raistrick et al.) | CVPR 2023, pp. 12630–12641 (CVF open access) | Fully procedural, implemented on **Blender** with no external assets | Shows that Blender's Python API can drive large procedural generation reproducibly. |
| Infinigen Indoors (Raistrick, Mei, Kayan et al.) | CVPR 2024, pp. 21783–21794 | Procedural indoor scenes, exportable to game engines | Precedent for procedural assets → real-time engines. |

## 5. What is appropriate for a solo developer on Apple Silicon

| Technique | Use in M11? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit meshes from footprints (extrusion, constrained triangulation) | **Yes, prototype 1** | Deterministic, testable without Blender (Shapely's constrained Delaunay handles holes and concave polygons), and exports to glTF with collision in Godot. |
| Split / CGA-style façade grammar in Python | **Yes, prototype 2** | Small rule set (floors → bays → window/door/balcony), runs on the CPU, and applies the occlusion idea to party walls. |
| Analytic gable and hip roofs on rectangles | **Yes** | No skeleton library needed. Irregular footprints get flat or skillion roofs, labelled as such. |
| Straight / weighted skeleton roofs | Later | Needs an implementation (none installed). Only worth it once simple roofs are validated. |
| Blender Eevee (Metal) | **Yes, iteration renders** | Headless render tested here: 2.4 s for a 256² test scene. |
| Blender Cycles (Metal GPU) | Final stills | Metal device detected (Apple M5 Pro, 16-core GPU). Slower; for a few final views. |
| Neural fields / diffusion 3D cities (CityDreamer, InfiniCity) | No | They produce non-mesh outputs and need large GPU training. Our goal is editable, traversable geometry. |
| Learned heights (Biljecki 2017; Milojevic-Dupont 2020) | Later, justified | Observed heights or levels exist for 42–98% of buildings in 7 of 9 cities (`outputs/m11/audit/height_audit.json`), so a learned model could be trained and validated. Start procedural, as specified. |
